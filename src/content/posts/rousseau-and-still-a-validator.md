title: 'Rousseau, and still a validator'
date: '2026-10-18'
excerpt: 'I believe people are good, and I build machines that check everything. One holds the other up.'
---

I believe people are good. I also build software that takes nobody's word for anything. My current project is a receptionist agent for a made-up clinic, and the whole point of it is that a stranger can replay the decisions it made and check them, step by step. Nothing in that system counts because someone feels it is true.

People hear these two things as a contradiction. For me one holds the other up.

### A belief without cases

The belief did not come from evidence. I cannot point to a moment when I watched someone choose good and decided that people are good. I understand a lot of things through feeling, and whenever I met a moment where people chose good, it felt right. That is what it rests on.

It also grew as resistance. Films, books and people kept telling the same story: humans are lazy and violent, so they need control. I always pushed back against it. Then I read Rutger Bregman's *Humankind*, and the feeling got its evidence. In the old argument between Hobbes and Rousseau, I side with Rousseau. One line from the book stays with me, in my own paraphrase: it is easy to believe people are bad, because then you do not have to live by virtue yourself.

### The root is fairness

So why build machines that check everything? Because underneath both is one wish. I want things to be fair. I want resources shared fairly, I want nobody to deceive anybody, I want justice. There is no contradiction inside me. The belief and the checking reinforce each other.

### A checker with no ego

The beauty of a blockchain, for me, is who does the checking. Not a person. Something with no ego. When the check belongs to a technology, the thought that you could cheat falls away, and that alone turns some people away from trying. It also removes a whole layer of what goes wrong between people: resentment, unfairness, bias.

Where does the resentment come from? We are all equal. When one person checks another, one of them now stands above and the other below, and everything else follows from that. A machine check keeps everyone level.

### Who writes the rules

The obvious objection lands on me, because I write such rules myself. Somebody has to write the code the machine checks by. Does that put the builder above everyone else?

What keeps it level is an agreement. Someone offers to write the code and the rules. The others agree or they do not. The community approves it or it does not, and once it is approved, the rule belongs to nobody.

Later I learned that this is close to Rousseau's other book, *The Social Contract* (1762), where a rule everyone agrees to belongs to no one: "each one, giving himself to all, gives himself to no one."

### The question I cannot answer yet

Here the idea is weakest. On Bittensor, the voices that count are not one person, one vote. They are weighted by stake: whoever holds more tokens has more say. That can put the rich back above everyone else, against the fairness I said sits at the root. Honestly, I do not have an answer to this yet. It is a point that deserves attention, and I would rather name it than cover it.

### Hospitality, and what takes its place

In Kyrgyzstan a stranger can knock on a door and be fed with no second thought. That is trust with no check at all. It comes from the time my ancestors were nomads. To survive in conditions that hard, we developed hospitality: if you see a stranger who is cold and hungry, you feed him. You secure survival not only for him but for your whole people, in case one of them ends up in the same state.

On a network the stranger is someone you will never meet. What binds people there, I think, is commitment to the idea of fairness. The whole movement around web3, blockchain and decentralization is built on the idea that we, the community, the people, matter more than control, repression and centralized management. That people are good and can cooperate and coordinate. Belief in that idea is what binds people in this field.

### What would make me wrong

Every day I meet news of violence against children, against women, against anyone at all. It demoralizes me and knocks me off my feet, especially when I picture the helpless person caught in its grip. And still I believe, and I want to believe, that people are good. Children are not born evil. They become it.

A child is a blank sheet. Whatever you draw on it, that is who the person becomes. Parents, surroundings, the state, the village, bad media, bad influence: all of that is what makes a person that way. What I build is a very technical thing. But I want to believe that by putting my work into what is fair, free and democratic, I touch the areas next to it too. Maybe not directly, but somehow.

### One sentence

The world makes it very easy to believe you do not matter. That you are small and cannot change anything. But a cliché is a cliché for a reason: even one person alone in the field is a warrior. What you do still changes things, and you have the strength and the means to have an effect. I want everyone to understand that they matter, they are needed, and they can.
